# Paper Review

## Review

Michael Costner's JAZB Framework v1.1 is a seven-publication governance architecture for agentic AI built around one proposition: technical capability does not constitute legitimate Authority. The framework converts that proposition into an institutional control model. An Enterprise Domain Authority represents the organization's highest active human Authority; proposed Law becomes governing only through a predefined multi-human authorization process; non-AI Law layers constrain AI interpretation; machine actors receive bounded, purpose-specific Authority; internal and border jurisdictions are separated; consequential decisions may require independent actuation; revocation and recovery are explicit lifecycle states; and Chains of Authority and Evidence are intended to make material actions reconstructable. Publication 7 consolidates this into 168 normative requirements and bounded conformance profiles.

The contribution is not another catalogue of responsible-AI principles. JAZB treats governance as runtime infrastructure. It distinguishes identity from Authority, permission from legitimacy, drafting from establishment, internal possession from external passage, anomaly from violation, and interpretation from execution. Version 1.1 also closes several common implementation gaps by requiring persistent behavioral state, authorized reset, effective-target and method validation, enforcement-path coverage, repeated-denial correlation, protected telemetry, and explicit handling of Governance Escape. Those mechanisms make revocation, delegation, evidence, and failure recovery operational rather than aspirational. The architecture is also disciplined about conformance boundaries: JAZB conformance is a claim about Authority architecture, not a claim of legal compliance, ethical adequacy, general security, or certification under NIST, ISO, CIS, or other frameworks.

Methodologically, this is a normative architecture and reference specification rather than an empirical study. The publication family develops doctrine, internal judicial mechanics, border governance, threat and recovery models, implementation guidance, standards mappings, and finally a canonical specification. Its evidentiary method is therefore architectural consistency and adversarial testability. The framework specifies negative cases that implementations should survive, including forged authorization, stale or substituted Law state, invalid delegation, cross-jurisdiction inheritance, effective-target redirection, unmediated consequential paths, unauthorized Point-state reset, evidence manipulation, border bypass, and conversion of compromised technical capability into unauthorized Authority. That is a meaningful step toward falsifiability, but the framework itself acknowledges that independent adversarial review, implementation validation, red-team exercises, and empirical measurement remain future work.

The central governance boundary lies at the root of sovereignty. JAZB is very good at proving that a machine action can be traced back through approved organizational Law and human authorization. It is much less complete on why the organization, its EDA, or its authorized human quorum should be treated as legitimate by people outside that chain who may still be affected by the resulting action. The framework states that organizations remain subject to external law, regulation, contract and fiduciary duty, and it carefully avoids claiming that provenance equals legal or ethical legitimacy. That scope defense is valid. It nevertheless means that the architecture solves provenance of organizational Authority more completely than legitimacy of organizational Authority. A discriminatory or procedurally defective rule can still be faithfully established, enforced, evidenced, and recovered.

This matters particularly because JAZB's judicial vocabulary can sound more institutionally complete than the mechanism actually is. The Judge, Judicial Instruments, Commissioner Law, Port Authority, and Enterprise AI Customs are organizational governance constructs, not substitutes for independent adjudication, public-law due process, or external remedy. The framework is explicit that external legal obligations continue to apply, but affected outsiders do not acquire a native JAZB contestation path merely because the internal governance chain is auditable. The strongest counterargument is that JAZB deliberately addresses enterprise Authority architecture, not universal legal legitimacy. That answer preserves the framework's coherence, but it fixes the correct claim boundary: JAZB can make delegated machine Authority bounded, revocable, reconstructable, and technically enforceable without by itself making the underlying organizational decision legitimate.

Relative to adjacent archive work on agentic normative infrastructure, responsibility cascades, rights-based agent governance, and governance evidence, JAZB advances the discussion by supplying a far more concrete execution model. It does not stop at saying that humans remain responsible. It specifies how human decisions become machine-executable Authority and how that Authority should fail closed, expire, revoke, survive compromise, and leave evidence. The next threshold is implementation evidence. Independent deployments should demonstrate that multi-human Law establishment resists collusion and capture, revocation reliably terminates delegated Authority, enforcement paths actually mediate consequential actions, evidence survives compromise, conformance assessments are reproducible, and external legal or rights constraints can be represented without collapsing them into organization-defined Law. If those tests succeed, JAZB would move from a detailed normative architecture toward a credible assurance regime for machine Authority.

## Key Insight

JAZB makes machine authority governable by turning provenance, delegation, revocation, jurisdiction, actuation, and evidence into explicit control objects, but organizational authority becomes legitimate governance only when the human sovereign root is itself constrained, contestable, and externally accountable.
